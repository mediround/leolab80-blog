Working with AI, I can try more things on my own. I create content, prepare replies to send to customers, and build features for the service. With the output right in front of me, it feels as if the business has grown just as much.

But the fact that things are piling up in front of me is separate from whether the business is doing well. For something to really be a business, customers need a reason to use it, and someone has to pay for it. You have to be able to keep doing the work with the money you receive. That doesn't change in the AI era either. In the end, a business is **delivering value to customers, getting paid for it, and keeping that work going.**

## A business doing well vs. doing a lot of work, fast

AI really does help a lot with work. [A study published in the *Quarterly Journal of Economics* in 2025](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658) analyzed 5,172 customer support agents at a software company. After an AI assistant tool was introduced, the number of customer issues resolved per hour rose by about 15% on average. Solving more customers' problems in the same amount of time makes it possible to reduce wait times and the burden on staff.

Research that looks at the business as a whole shows a different side, too. [A study published in *Management Science* in 2026](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909) ran a randomized experiment with 640 small business owners in Kenya over about five months. Some of them were given a GPT-4-based business advice tool, but the study could not statistically confirm an average improvement in sales or profits overall.

Doing an already-defined customer support job better is different from applying AI's advice to your own business. Even as AI gets more capable, the business owner still has to decide what to hand off and how that work connects to customers and sales. It's genuinely welcome that AI can create marketing content, but that content only has real value if it solves a problem for the customer who sees it or leads them to take some action. If you make 100 pieces of content a day and they don't contribute to sales, what's the point?

## It turns out not every cost AI cuts becomes my profit

When AI lowers the cost of translation and content production, there's room to improve profits. You can also put AI on customer consultations and save on labor costs. Adding more channels for receiving inquiries doesn't add much of a burden. But competitors use similar tools, too.

So if competitors get similar efficiency from AI, the core of competition now becomes what service we offer that carries value only we can provide. The fact that you got paid isn't enough to call it a good business. In the end, a business is **delivering value to customers, getting paid for it, and keeping that work going, so value has to come first, before money.**
